# Lessons from the source project

Each rule in the skill exists because something like this went wrong.

- **Misquotes survive the first review.** Two on-screen quotes had been "tidied" and changed meaning; only the adversarial cut review against the dossier caught them. Copy quotes character for character, typos included.
- **Claims drift stronger than sources.** "Every joke is true" became "based on real chat history, some scenes rebuilt"; a one-repo number read as an account-wide verdict; a raw local count read as "he wrote". Word each claim as narrowly as its source.
- **Reviewers find invented details.** A small "attempt 1" was added for rhythm; no attempt 2 existed. Nothing on screen without a source row.
- **Merged quotes are fabrications.** Two compliments from different dates shown as one message was a fake transcript. Show two dated cards.
- **Models draw gibberish text.** Generate every surface blank and draw words in code.
- **Tags cover the face.** Captions and banners sat on the subject's eyes in the best shots. Check positions on real frames.
- **Batch after a motion test.** Two clips first; releasing all clips blind risks the whole budget on one bad prompt.
- **One corrective sentence per retake.** Rewriting the whole prompt changed things that were fine.
- **The song decides the length.** The brief asked for a film well under the length the song turned out to be.
- **Speech-to-text mishears sung words.** Check every word time by ear or against the lyrics.
- **Rulings can carry the same typo as the plan.** Re-check a ruling's wording against the source before applying it.
- **Context is not universal.** Jargon counts mean nothing to strangers; add one plain tag (for example "one computer only") rather than a paragraph.
